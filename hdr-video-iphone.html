Common questions

HDR video, without the mystery.

Which iPhones can record HDR with Sideline Save?

HDR depends on the iPhone model, active camera format, frame rate, and video output support. Sideline Save checks those capabilities at runtime, so the app uses HDR where the capture path supports it and SDR elsewhere.

Is HDR the same as 4K?

No. Resolution describes how many pixels the video has. HDR describes the range of brightness and color the video can preserve. They can work together, but one does not guarantee the other.

Will every person see my video in HDR?

No. The viewer's phone, display, app, and any later editing or re-encoding step all matter. Compatible Apple displays and supported Instagram Reels workflows can show HDR; other paths may show a standard-range version.

What happens when HDR is not available?

Sideline Save saves the clip as SDR. The app keeps the capture reliable and reports the clip's actual dynamic range in its saved-video metadata.

Do I need to change a setting before recording?

No. HDR is not a separate capture-screen toggle in Sideline Save. When the active iPhone format and video output support HDR, the app selects it automatically.
